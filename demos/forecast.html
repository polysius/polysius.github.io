Baseline forecasting playground

From the load forecasting project. Two baselines forecast the next 24 hours from the last 168. Which one wins depends on how far ahead you look.

Synthetic data. The load curve is generated in your browser from a daily cycle, a weekend dip and correlated noise. It is not the project's real data and it does not run the project's LSTM. It only shows why the two baselines behave as they do. Real results are on the project page.

  • History (168 h)
  • Actual next 24 h
  • Naive: repeat the last value
  • Seasonal naive: same hour yesterday

Average error over 300 random curves

Mean absolute error at three horizons, using the settings above. Lower is better. The best baseline at each horizon is in bold.

Repeating the last value wins very close in and falls behind as the horizon grows. In the real study persistence also led at 1 and 6 hours (MASE 0.473 and 0.644), seasonal naive scored 0.743 at 24 hours, and the LSTM was worse than seasonal naive at every horizon. Push the level shift away from zero to see both baselines fail when the regime changes.
